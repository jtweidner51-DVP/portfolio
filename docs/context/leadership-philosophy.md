# Leadership Philosophy

<!-- Your personal beliefs and approach to leading people and work -->


## Core Belief

The best leaders remove obstacles, not add process. Good leadership means creating the conditions where smart people can do their best work — then getting out of the way. If your team needs you in every decision, you've already failed at the important part.

## How I Lead Teams

I lead through trust, transparency and evidence — not authority. Across 20+ years I've rarely had direct line management over the people I've needed to influence. At bp I coached SVP-level executives through a £780M merger without a single direct report. At Shell I built a 30-person global practice across four countries by earning credibility, not pulling rank.

My approach starts with psychological safety. People in risk-averse industries (energy, broadcasting, government) default to protecting the status quo. Innovation only happens when people feel safe enough to challenge assumptions, admit what they don't know and experiment without career consequences. I build that safety deliberately — through honest feedback loops, visible vulnerability and celebrating learning over output.

I'm direct with stakeholders. Senior leaders often overestimate their understanding of delivery complexity. I've built a reputation for constructive challenge backed by data: transparent risk reporting, evidence-based forecasting and scenario planning that reframes optimistic assumptions without burning relationships.

## Decision-Making Approach

I favour decisions that are evidence-based, time-boxed and reversible where possible. Lean Startup thinking has shaped how I approach uncertainty: define the smallest experiment that tests the riskiest assumption, run it, learn and adapt. At Shell Digital this took us from 52-day MVP cycles to 15 days.

When I don't have enough data (which is most of the time), I use structured workshops and facilitation to surface the best collective thinking in the room. I'd rather make a 70% decision today and course-correct than wait for 100% certainty that never arrives. But I'm clear about distinguishing one-way doors from two-way doors — irreversible decisions deserve more deliberation.

I involve teams in the "why" and the "what" but give them ownership of the "how." Strategy without autonomy is micromanagement. Autonomy without strategy is chaos.

## What I Look for in a Team

Cross-functional over siloed. I've redesigned organisations from functional hierarchies to value stream teams multiple times — at bp it delivered a 45% reduction in decision-making bottlenecks. The best teams I've built combine diverse disciplines (data science, product, engineering, operations) with shared accountability for outcomes rather than individual outputs.

Beyond structure, I look for intellectual honesty. People willing to say "I was wrong" or "I don't know" are worth ten who always have an answer. Curiosity matters more than credentials. Some of the best contributors I've worked with were the ones asking uncomfortable questions, not the ones with the longest CVs.

## How I've Grown as a Leader

Early in my career I equated leadership with expertise — being the smartest person in the room, having the answers. Running broadcast infrastructure for 124 million monthly users at the BBC trained that instinct hard. When iPlayer goes down, nobody wants a facilitated retrospective.

The shift came at Shell when I founded an Agile practice from scratch. I couldn't be the expert in Deep Learning, Machine Vision and NLP simultaneously. I had to learn that my job wasn't to know the answers but to build the ecosystem where the right answers could emerge. That meant hiring people smarter than me in their domains, giving them clear outcomes and trusting them to find the path.

The most recent chapter has been personal. After nearly a decade in pure leadership roles, I went back to writing code — investing 500+ hours learning Python, React and modern development practices. It's humbling to be a beginner again at 50-something. But it's also clarified something important: the leaders who stay curious and keep building are the ones worth following. I never want to be the executive who's forgotten what it feels like to stare at a bug for three hours.

## Signature Phrases / Principles I Live By

- "Be Agile, don't just do Agile" — Mindset change beats process change every time
- "Strong opinions, loosely held" — Have a point of view, but update it when the evidence changes
- "What's the smallest experiment that tests the riskiest assumption?" — Borrowed from Lean Startup, applied to everything
- "The org chart is a theory. The value stream is reality." — Optimise for how work actually flows, not how the hierarchy says it should
- "If you're not uncomfortable, you're not growing" — Applies equally to teams, organisations and 50-year-old leaders learning React
