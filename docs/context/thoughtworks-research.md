## Current Areas of Focus

**AI Portfolio Governance at Enterprise Scale.** The challenge has shifted from "can we build AI?" to "how do we govern a portfolio of AI initiatives responsibly?" This is where my Shell Digital experience (governing 20+ production AI products across Deep Learning, Machine Vision, NLP and Automated Reasoning) meets the current explosion in agentic AI. I'm actively studying how portfolio governance needs to evolve when the "products" in your portfolio are autonomous agents with tool access and decision-making capability.

**Agentic AI Workflows and MCP.** The Model Context Protocol is rapidly becoming the integration standard for how AI agents interact with external tools and data sources. I'm tracking the security implications (toxic flow analysis, the "lethal trifecta" of private data access, untrusted content and external communication capability) and the governance patterns that enterprise leaders need to understand before deploying agentic systems at scale.

**Hands-on Technical Reskilling.** Over the past 12+ months I've invested 500+ hours returning to hands-on development: Python, Django, React, modern web development, RAG architectures and data engineering fundamentals. I built a RAG pipeline (LangChain, ChromaDB, GPT-4 Turbo) that turns career documents into a searchable AI knowledge base with cited answers, confidence scores and cost tracking. I built my portfolio website from scratch in React with Vite. This isn't nostalgia — it's deliberate capability building so I can govern AI portfolios with technical authority rather than slide-deck confidence.

**Green Software and Sustainable AI.** Certified Green Software Practitioner (Green Software Foundation, March 2026). The carbon cost of AI workloads is a material consideration in energy sector programmes, not an academic one. When you're running AI programmes for a company that's simultaneously trying to reach net zero, the environmental impact of your compute decisions is part of the delivery equation.

## Thoughtworks / Industry Research

**Thoughtworks LookingGlass 2026** — The central argument resonates deeply with my experience: "Operationalising AI is no longer about scaling models — it's about designing organisations where intelligence can flow." This is exactly what I spent a decade doing at Shell and bp. The organisational rewiring — psychological safety, cultural intelligence, facilitative governance — is where AI programmes succeed or fail. The technology is rarely the bottleneck.

**Thoughtworks Technology Radar (Volume 33, November 2025)** — I track the Radar closely because it represents practitioner-validated opinion rather than vendor marketing. Key themes from the latest edition that align with my trajectory: infrastructure orchestration arriving for AI (GPU-aware scheduling becoming table stakes), the rise of agents elevated by MCP, AI coding workflows transforming software delivery, and emerging AI antipatterns (complacency with AI-generated code, naive API-to-MCP conversion, AI-accelerated shadow IT). Claude Code at Trial is personally relevant — I use it daily as part of my development workflow.

**"Organisational Zombies" — Internal Whitepaper (bp, 2024).** I wrote an internal whitepaper at bp on the pattern of programmes that continue consuming resources despite being strategically dead — programmes where the leadership layer is too protected to receive honest feedback about viability. I presented it to leadership. They acknowledged it and buried it. The irony was not lost on me. The underlying research drew on Edmondson's psychological safety work and systems thinking on feedback loops in hierarchical organisations.

**Green Software Foundation.** Thoughtworks co-founded the GSF in 2021. My membership and certification align with a conviction that sustainable software engineering isn't a nice-to-have — it's a core competency for anyone governing AI portfolios in the energy sector. The carbon cost of training and inference workloads should be a first-class consideration in portfolio governance, alongside cost, risk and strategic alignment.

## Technology Radar Opinions

**What I'd put at Adopt:** Lean Portfolio Management for AI governance. Communities of Practice as knowledge flow infrastructure (the Radar's "knowledge flows over knowledge stocks" resonates). Pre-commit hooks and continuous compliance — essential hygiene as AI-generated code volumes increase.

**What I'd put at Trial:** MCP as the integration standard for agentic AI (with security caveats). Context engineering as a discipline — it's the next evolution beyond prompt engineering and directly relevant to how AI portfolios manage information flow. Curated shared instructions for software teams — I've been doing a version of this with Transformation Playbooks for years.

**What I'd put at Assess:** Small language models for narrow agentic tasks (most enterprise AI use cases don't need frontier models). Spec-driven development (fascinating but the workflows are still too elaborate). LLM observability platforms like DataDog LLM Observability and LangSmith — essential for any production AI portfolio but still maturing.

**What I'd put at Hold:** Naive API-to-MCP conversion (exposing internal APIs directly to autonomous agents without purpose-built governance is asking for trouble). Text-to-SQL in unsupervised workflows (hallucination risk is too high without human review). Standalone data engineering teams (the siloed model is an antipattern that repeats past mistakes — embed data engineers in cross-functional teams following data mesh principles). Complacency with AI-generated code — speed without quality discipline is faster technical debt.

## External Influence & Sources

**Amy Edmondson — The Fearless Organization.** The research on psychological safety is the single most important thing I've read for my leadership practice. Every transformation I've led at Shell and bp was fundamentally about creating the conditions for teams to experiment, fail and learn without fear. The 350% MVP acceleration at Shell and the 10% engagement improvement at bp were psychological safety outcomes before they were process outcomes.

**Erin Meyer — The Culture Map.** Essential reading for anyone leading global programmes. I use Meyer's eight dimensions (communication, feedback, decision-making, trust, scheduling, disagreeing, leading, persuading) as a practical diagnostic tool, not just a conceptual framework. The Culture Map session I ran with bp's M&T leadership team — where long-tenured UK leaders were confidently wrong about how their colleagues in China and Bangalore operated — was one of the most impactful interventions of my career.

**Roger Schwarz — The Skilled Facilitator / Smart Leaders, Smarter Teams.** The Mutual Learning Model and 8 facilitative behaviours are my operating system for leadership without authority. Schwarz's core insight — that culture change happens through leaders' behaviours, not through presentations or mandated processes — is the thread that runs through everything I do.

**Dave Snowden — Cynefin Framework.** Changed how I think about problem categorisation. Most AI transformation challenges sit in the complex domain (probe-sense-respond) but get treated as if they're complicated (analyse-plan-execute). This mismatch is one of the most common failure modes I see in enterprise AI programmes.

**Gene Kim — The Phoenix Project / The Unicorn Project.** The value stream thinking and flow-based delivery principles directly influenced how I approach portfolio governance and organisational design. The concept of the "five ideals" from The Unicorn Project (locality, flow, improvement, psychological safety, customer focus) maps closely to my own framework.

**Thoughtworks Technology Radar.** I've tracked the Radar for years because it represents what practitioners actually experience, not what vendors want you to believe. It's one of the few industry publications that puts things on "Hold" with clear reasoning — that intellectual honesty is rare and valuable.

**Green Software Foundation / Sustainable Web Manifesto.** The intersection of software engineering and environmental impact is a growing area of focus. As AI workloads consume increasingly significant compute resources, the carbon cost of inference and training is no longer something portfolio leaders can ignore.
